# Module 2: Security Principles

The CIA triad is the core model of information assurance. It names three goals: confidentiality, integrity, and availability. A failure in any one of them is a security failure.

Confidentiality is protected with controls such as encryption and access control. Integrity is protected with hashing, digital signatures, and change control. Availability is protected with backups, redundancy, and disaster recovery.

Beyond the triad, authentication proves who a user is, authorization decides what they may do, and non-repudiation stops someone from denying an action they took.

- **Confidentiality**: only authorized people can see the data
- **Integrity**: data stays accurate and is not changed without permission
- **Availability**: systems and data can be reached when they are needed
- **Authentication**: proving that a user is who they claim to be
- **Least privilege**: giving users only the access they need for their work
